# INC-2024-033: Kerberoasting Sweep Against Service Accounts

## Summary

"Potential Kerberoasting via RC4 Service Ticket Request" fired repeatedly within a five-minute window. Investigation traced the requests to a low-privilege web-application account on WEB1.insecurebank.local that had been compromised via a deserialization vulnerability in the banking portal, and was then used to enumerate and request RC4 service tickets for every SPN-bearing account in the domain.

## Timeline

- **T+0:00** — Attacker exploits a deserialization vulnerability in the customer banking portal, gaining code execution as the low-privilege `svc-webapp` account.
- **T+0:08** — `svc-webapp` used to run SPN enumeration (`setspn`-equivalent LDAP queries).
- **T+0:11** — Rapid RC4 (0x17) service ticket requests observed for over 40 distinct SPNs within 90 seconds; alert fires on request volume.
- **T+0:19** — SOC isolates WEB1 and disables `svc-webapp`.
- **T+4:00** — Forensic review finds no evidence any offline-cracked ticket was successfully used before containment, but several targeted accounts had weak/legacy passwords that would likely have cracked quickly.

## Root Cause

The deserialization vulnerability in the banking portal was the initial access vector; the Kerberoasting sweep was straightforward opportunistic follow-on activity enabled by the environment's inconsistent enforcement of AES-only Kerberos encryption (only one account, `svc-legacy-invoice`, was documented as an expected RC4 exception — the sweep surfaced dozens of other accounts still capable of RC4 tickets).

## Impact

No confirmed credential cracking or misuse before containment. However, the sweep exposed that AES enforcement was far less consistent across service accounts than assumed, representing latent risk beyond this specific incident.

## Remediation

1. Patched the banking portal's deserialization vulnerability.
2. Disabled and rotated `svc-webapp`.
3. Enforced AES256-only Kerberos encryption domain-wide except for the single documented legacy exception (`svc-legacy-invoice`), closing the RC4 exposure surfaced by this sweep.

## Lessons Learned

- A single Kerberoasting alert on request volume caught what would otherwise have been a slow, low-and-slow credential-harvesting campaign — volume-based detection remains valuable even against a well-resourced attacker who could have throttled requests to blend in.
- Documented encryption exceptions (like `svc-legacy-invoice`) need periodic validation that they're still the *only* exceptions, not just documentation that goes stale.
